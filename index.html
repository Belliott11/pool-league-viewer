Season Rates (Individual)

Every counting stat here (PTS, OREB, DREB, AST, STL, BLK, TOV, PF, Pts Allowed, Beaten, Stops, Def Rating) is a rate per 20 combined points scored in the game, not a season total or a per-game average — only games with real shots logged and equal team sizes count toward these at all, so a game that's just been rostered but not reviewed yet (or a 3-on-2 that skews the whole game's competitive shape) doesn't drag anyone's numbers down. Games are capped at different totals (16 or 21), so a plain per-game average isn't a fair comparison between a player who mostly plays 16-point games and one who mostly plays 21s — the game's combined final score stands in for "how much game happened," since possessions aren't tracked. Includes shooting splits and defensive/advanced numbers derived from each game's Shot Log — see the Game Stats table in Stat Entry for what each one means. Off Rating is offense-only Game Score — PTS, shooting efficiency, rebounds, assists, TOV, and fouls, adapted from the standard basketball Game Score formula minus its STL and BLK terms. Def Rating is STL, plus BLK (only when it isn't already one of this player's own Stops, so a blocked-and-tagged shot isn't credited twice), plus Stops minus Beaten minus 0.4×Pts Allowed — this tool's stand-in for the NBA stat of the same name, since real Defensive Rating needs possession counts this tool doesn't track. 0 for anyone never tagged as a defender with no steals or blocks, not a penalty for conservative tagging. Two-Way is Off Rating plus Def Rating. Click a player's name for their full game log and head-to-head history. Click a column header to sort by it (click again to flip direction). Shot%/AST%/OREB%/DREB%/TRB%/TOV% are hidden by default — click below to show them (still always in the CSV export either way). Include Outlier Games (on by default, matching the original always-been-true behavior) drops a player's own statistical outlier games from their rates when turned off — for each player with at least 4 qualifying games, any single game whose Two-Way/20 falls outside 1.5x their own IQR (computed fresh from their own games, not a fixed cutoff) is set aside, so one absurdly good or bad night doesn't dominate a small-sample average. This reaches every per-player stat (this table, Two-Way Trend, Teammate Synergy/Quality, both Matchup Difficulty charts) but deliberately not panels that pool many players within the same game (League Heatmap, Matchup Grid, Head-to-Head, Balance Teams' chemistry/win-rate) — an outlier for one player isn't necessarily one for everyone else who was in that same game. With fewer than 4 qualifying games, nothing gets excluded regardless of the toggle — there isn't enough data yet to call anything an outlier.

Past Seasons (League)

The league-wide equivalent of each player's own Past Seasons panel on Player Detail — full standings for one closed season at a time, computed live off that season's own archived games (not a frozen snapshot, so these stay correct even if a stat's formula changes later), shown regardless of the Include Past Seasons toggle above. That toggle blends past seasons into the live current-season totals; this instead shows one past season completely on its own, the way it actually played out. Pick a season, click a name to jump to their Player Detail page.

Consistency

Standard deviation of each player's own per-game Two-Way/20 (the same numbers their Two-Way Trend chart on Player Detail plots) — lower means steadier output night to night, not necessarily better output. A player who's reliably average every game reads as more consistent here than a boom-or-bust one who's spectacular half the time and poor the other half, even if their season average lands the same as someone steadier. Needs at least 2 qualifying games — a single game has no variance to measure. Worth knowing: with Include Outlier Games turned off above, this measures the spread of a player's typical games with their own most extreme night(s) already set aside — not their true overall variance including the outlier. One of the real voted MVP award's own criteria (see Awards vs. Stats below, where twoWayTotal — season-long Two-Way, volume included — is already the closest tracked comparison to that award) — kept as its own ranking rather than folded into a made-up combined score, since "consistent" and "valuable overall" are different questions worth answering separately.

Player Comparison

Pick any two players to see their full stat lines side by side — every column from the table above, including the advanced % ones, whether or not they're currently shown there. Green marks whichever value is better for a given stat where "better" has a clear direction (lower for TOV/PF/Pts Allowed/Opp FG%/Beaten/TOV%, higher for everything else with a real quality read) — left uncolored for GP and the shot-share percentages (Shot%/AST%/OREB%/DREB%/TRB%), since a bigger share of the team's shots or assists reflects a role, not necessarily better play.

Power Ranking vs. Performance

A "power ranking" isn't voted or tracked by this tool — it's the real site's own per-night rank/percentile for each party (rank 1 = best that night, percentile field-size-normalized: 100 = first, 0 = last), pulled from the season spreadsheet as a frozen historical record. Paired here with that same player's actual Two-Way/20 in just the games logged for that date, computed live. Only nights with at least one reviewed game show up here at all — the other 10 real parties predate any footage existing, and a night with footage nobody's gotten to reviewing yet is left out too, rather than showing a table of nothing but dashes. A player with no games logged for that specific night still shows "—", not a zero.

Two-Way Quadrant

Off Rating/20 (offense) vs. Def Rating/20 for every player who's played, plotted separately instead of pre-summed into Two-Way Score — so "who's good" splits into "good at what." Quadrant lines are drawn at zero on each axis.

Volume vs. Efficiency

Offense only, separate from the Two-Way Quadrant above — pure shot volume (FGA/20) on the x-axis against season TS% on the y-axis, so a high-volume/low-efficiency player and a low-volume/high-efficiency player show up as mirror opposites directly, instead of needing someone to cross-reference the FGA and TS% columns on the main table by hand.

League Shot Heatmap

Every marked field goal, league-wide, bucketed by court zone — color is FG% in that zone (red low, green high), the number is attempts. A zone stays blank until at least one shot lands in it. Only shots with a marked location are included (see Export → Backfill Shot Locations to fill in the rest).

Two-Way/20 Rank Over the Season

One line per player, tracking where they'd rank if the season had ended at each date shown — a checkpoint exists for every date with at least one qualifying game, and a player's rank there comes from their cumulative Two-Way/20 across every qualifying game up through that date (not just that date's own games), ranked against everyone else's own cumulative number at that same moment. Rank 1 is at the top, so a climbing line means genuinely improving standing, not just a good single night. Each point is the same colored avatar used everywhere else in the app, with that checkpoint's rank number inside it instead of an initial. Built on the same games Include Imbalanced Games/Include Past Seasons already gate — deliberately not on Include Outlier Games, since a per-player outlier exclusion would mean two players could disagree about which dates even count as checkpoints.

Shot Distance

Every field goal splits further by how far it actually was from the hoop, on both sides of the 3pt line — Close and Midrange split the 2PT zone at its midpoint; Line (a normal, makeable three right at the line — Poolean's three is straight, not a curved arc, hence "Line" not "Arc") and Deep (a much lower-percentage near-pool-length heave) split the 3PT zone the same way the plain 3PT% on the table above blends together. Both breakpoints are radial distance from the hoop against a single adjustable constant (`CLOSE_RANGE_THRESHOLD` and `THREE_PT_DEEP_THRESHOLD` in app.js) — the 3PT one drawn from an early season sample, the 2PT one a rougher starting guess with no shot volume behind it yet, so treat it as even more provisional. Each zone column shows FG% there (click to sort); the small percentage beneath it is that zone's share of the player's own attempts — how well they shoot from there, and how much they lean on it, in one cell instead of two separate panels. The Mix column is the same shot-selection breakdown as a quick visual (color tracks typical shot quality: green close, amber midrange, teal at the line, red deep). Only field goals with a marked shot location count (see Export → Backfill Shot Locations to fill in the rest). Treat any one player's split as an early read, not a verdict, until more games are logged.

True Shooting %, league-wide, for each of the four Shot Distance bands — the same numbers as the Shot Distance table above, laid out so "shooting gets worse with distance" (or wherever it actually breaks down) lands in one glance instead of a table someone has to compare in their head. League TS% Over Time

True Shooting % pooled across every player, per date with a reviewed game — a single league-wide efficiency line, useful for spotting drift over the season or checking the effect of a future rule change. Not a player-comparison stat.

Wide-Open Shooting

Every field goal attempt with no tagged defender at all — the opposite end of the Matchup Grid above. Free throws are excluded entirely (they're uncontested by rule, not by circumstance, so folding them in would trivially inflate the numbers). Share of FGA is how much of a player's own shot diet was untagged, for context on how much signal the TS% column actually carries. Click any column header to sort.

Head-to-Head Matchup Grid

Every scorer down one axis, every defender across the other — the league-wide extension of the per-player Head-to-Head tables on Player Detail, which only ever show one player's matchups at a time. Each cell is that scorer's FG% against that specific defender (color follows the same red-low/green-high scale as the heatmap above, opacity ramping up with sample size). A blank cell means that pairing has never been tagged. Rows and columns are sorted by attempts, most data first. Not filtered to field goals only — same behavior as the Head-to-Head tables it extends.

Teammate Lift Matrix

The pairwise With/Without comparison the Best Teammate award's Average Teammate Lift boils into one number per player — here as a full grid instead: row player on the team, column player's own Two-Way/20 change as a result (green = boosts them, red = drags them down; opacity tracks how large the swing is relative to the biggest one on the grid). A blank cell means that pairing hasn't logged games both with and without each other yet. This grid is not symmetric — row A / column B ("does A help B") and row B / column A ("does B help A") are two different facts about two different people's games, not mirror images of each other.

Teammate Context

The season-average headline numbers from Player Detail's Teammate Quality, Offensive/Defensive Matchup Difficulty, and Assisted By panels, side by side for every player at once — built to catch a specific pattern without clicking into each player one at a time: someone whose own Off Rating/20 leans heavily on strong teammates (high Teammate Quality), easy defenders to shoot over (low Off Matchup Difficulty), and light defensive assignments (low Def Matchup Difficulty) is a different case from someone producing the same number on their own. Teammate Quality is teammates' season Off Rating/20; Off Matchup Difficulty is the season Def Rating/20 of whoever defended this player's shots (how good those defenders have been defensively); Def Matchup Difficulty is the season Off Rating/20 of whoever this player defended (how good those scorers have been offensively) — see the Player Detail panels for the full explanation and the game-by-game trend charts. Click a column header to sort by it.

Assist Connections

The top passer-to-scorer connections, league-wide, by assist count — who's set up whom the most. Directional: Alice assisting Bob is a separate row from Bob assisting Alice, since those are different facts. Trimmed to the same number of rows as Teammate Context next to it, just so the two panels line up at roughly the same height — not a meaningful cutoff on its own. See a player's own Assisted By panel on Player Detail for their full, untrimmed list of connections, or Export → Download Assist Connections CSV for every connection league-wide.

Out-of-Bounds Misses

How often a player's own missed shot ends up out of bounds — per Poolean's rule, whoever last touched the ball loses possession, so this is really a "how often does the ball leave their hands for good on a miss" read, not a shooting-accuracy stat. Only misses count as the denominator (a make can't go out of bounds). Sorted by number of misses.

Second-Chance Conversion

"True" second-chance conversion, not just who grabs offensive rebounds — an OREB counts as converted when, within 20 seconds of the missed shot's own video timestamp, either that rebounder scored themselves or someone else scored with them credited as the assist (either path counts, a kick-out is worth the same as a putback). Both the miss and the scoring follow-up need a real video timestamp to be checked; an OREB logged without one still counts toward the total but can't be evaluated for conversion. The 20-second window is a single adjustable constant (`SECOND_CHANCE_WINDOW_SECONDS` in app.js), same pattern as the shot-distance thresholds. This panel runs the same algorithm as scripts/second-chance-analysis.js, just against whatever's loaded in this browser instead of an exported file.

Game-Winning Buckets

How many times a player's shot was the one that actually closed out a game their team won — the real game-ending basket in a race-to-a-target format. Worth being precise about what this is: in a race-to-target format with no clock, the last basket of every decided game is *by definition* scored by the winning team — so this isn't tracking rare, buzzer-beater moments, it's a "who tends to be the one closing games out" tally that grows by exactly one per fully-timestamped decided game. That's exactly why the Clutch comparison above is built on Close-Game Shooting instead of this — still a real signal here (it correlates with who's on the floor and scoring late in wins), just not a scarce one. Only credited when that specific shot has a real video timestamp; without one, there's no trustworthy way to say it was really the last shot of the game, so the game is skipped rather than guessed at. A season count, not a rate.

Close-Game Shooting

This is the tracked stat behind the Clutch comparison above — a margin-aware read, not Game-Winning Buckets, since GWB is explicitly non-scarce by construction (the last basket of every decided game belongs to the winning team, full stop) and measures "who tends to close games out," not performance under real pressure. This instead is TS%, but only counting attempts from games that actually finished close: decided by 5 points or fewer (`CLUTCH_MARGIN_THRESHOLD` in app.js, a starting guess, not a value backed by real season margin data yet). Tied games count here even though a tie has no "winning shot" for GWB to credit. Click any column header to sort.

Best & Worst Individual Games

Every player-game ranked by that single game's actual Two-Way score (Off Rating + Defensive Rating) — deliberately not a per-20 rate or a season total, since the rest of this page normalizes for fair player-to-player comparison and that averages away exactly the thing this panel exists to surface: one specific night's story, buried otherwise inside that game's own box score. Every player on either roster for a reviewed game gets a row, even a quiet one. On a thin season the two lists are capped so they never show the same handful of games twice, just reversed.

Teammate Synergy (With/Without)

For each teammate this player has shared a team with, this player's own per-20 numbers in games with that teammate on their side vs. games without them (opposing team or not playing) — only games with real shots logged count either way. "—" means there's no game in that bucket yet to compare against. A small GP on either side makes the comparison noisy, not a verdict.

Teammate Quality

Average season Off Rating/20 of this player's own teammates (not this player), game by game, with a dashed season-average line. Off Rating specifically, not Two-Way — what this measures is a teammate's offensive gravity: how much they draw defensive attention, create mismatches, or set up easy looks, not their own defense. Doesn't correct for this player also counting toward each teammate's own Off Rating/20 (no leave-one-out adjustment) — a simplification, same tradeoff every other season-long number on this page already makes.

Assisted By

What share of this player's own made field goals were set up by someone else, and how good (season Off Rating/20) those passers have been — the direct test of "getting fed easy looks by good players" vs. creating shots alone. Field goals only; free throws don't carry an assist by rule.

Offensive Matchup Difficulty

Average season Def Rating/20 of whoever was tagged defending this player's own shot attempts, game by game, with a dashed season-average line — the mirror of Defensive Matchup Difficulty below, from the scorer's side: how tough have the defenders this player shoots over actually been? A double-teamed shot counts toward every tagged defender, not split, same rule Stops/Beaten/Pts Allowed already use. A wide-open (untagged) shot contributes nothing — there's no defender to rate.

Defensive Matchup Difficulty

Average season Off Rating/20 of whoever this player was tagged defending, game by game, with a dashed season-average line — the defensive-side counterpart to Teammate Quality above: does this player draw the tough offensive assignments, or get sheltered on weaker ones? Weighted per shot, not per opponent — facing the same tough scorer five times in one game counts five times, the same way Stops/Beaten/Pts Allowed already weight every tagged shot equally.
