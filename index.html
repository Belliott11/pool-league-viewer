Comparison & Trends

Player Comparison

Pick two players to see every stat side by side. Green marks the better number where better is clear, like fewer turnovers. Share stats aren't colored, since they reflect a role, not quality.

vs.

Awards vs. Stats

Each voted award next to the closest stat from logged games. The vote and the numbers are allowed to disagree; that's the point of comparing them.

Power Ranking vs. Performance

Each party night's real power ranking next to how that player actually played in that night's logged games. Only nights with logged film show up, and games stopped early are left out.

Two-Way Quadrant

Off Rating/20 (offense) vs. Def Rating/20 for every player who's played, plotted separately instead of pre-summed into Two-Way Score, so "who's good" splits into "good at what." Quadrant lines are drawn at zero on each axis.

Volume vs. Efficiency

How much each player shoots (attempts per 20) against how efficiently (TS%). High-volume and high-efficiency players end up in opposite corners.

TS% by Shot Distance

League-wide scoring efficiency at each shot distance, to see where shooting drops off. Free throws and shots without a marked spot aren't included.

League TS% Over Time

True Shooting % pooled across every player, per date with a reviewed game: a single league-wide efficiency line, useful for spotting drift over the season or checking the effect of a future rule change. Not a player-comparison stat.

League Shot Heatmap

Every marked shot, grouped by court zone. Color is FG% (red low, green high) and the number is attempts. Backfill Shot Locations in Export fills in unmarked shots.

Two-Way/20 Rank Over the Season

Where each player would rank if the season had ended on each date, based on every game up to then. A rising line means their standing is improving, not just one good night.

Closed Seasons in This App (League)

Full standings for one closed season at a time. Pick a season, and click a name for their page. The Include Past Seasons switch above blends old seasons into the current numbers instead.

Consistency

How much each player's game-to-game output swings. Lower is steadier, not necessarily better. Needs 2+ games. With Outlier Games off, each player's most extreme nights are left out.

Defensive Shot Distance

The same zones for shots this player defended: how well opponents shoot against them from each distance. Free throws aren't included. Early numbers are small.

Shot Type Efficiency

How each player's shots were created: catch-and-shoot, drive, move (spin, hesitation or pump fake), deep heave, or dunk. Each shows TS% and share of shots once there are 5 of that type. Tag older shots in Export, Review Shot Types.

Deep Shot Check

How much of the league's deep 3-point volume is the rushed heave off a check or rebound, compared with a deep shot taken after a real possession, and how each one goes. Only tagged deep shots count, and at least 10 are needed to show a split.

Is the Move Working?

For players with 5+ Move shots, compares TS% on those against their other shots. A gap of 10 points or more counts as working or trailing. Samples are small, so treat it as a first look.

Shot Types: Open vs. Contested

Each shot type open versus guarded (a percentage shows once there are 5 shots). The second table compares catch-and-shoots at matching distances, since open shots tend to be longer.

Rebound Battle Record

Who wins contested rebounds. A win is grabbing it while someone fights you for it; a loss is fighting for it and not getting it. Contest Rate is how many of their rebounds were contested. Needs 5+ contests.

Each player's contested-rebound win rate against each other player. Fainter cells have fewer battles, and blank means they've never battled. Only players with 5+ contests appear.

Teammate Lift Matrix

How much each player (row) lifts or drags each teammate's (column) Two-Way/20. Green helps, red hurts. Blank means not enough games with and without each other. Row and column aren't mirror images.

Second-Chance Points Allowed

How often a defender who loses a contested rebound then gives up points. Lower is better. Rows under 5 situations are marked as small samples. Needs video times.

Close-Game Shooting

Close-Game Defense

Game-Winning Buckets

Shot Arc Hand-Labeling

Label the ball in shot clips to help train ball tracking. Click the ball's center on each frame (skipped frames are filled in), or No ball visible (Space). Mark Shot Start and End to trim the clip, or Not a valid shot for dunks and mixed plays. Download the labels when done.

Review Possible Dunks

Close/midrange 2PT field goals logged before shots could be marked as dunks, so the ball-flight tracking work can exclude real dunks. Click Watch film to check, then Dunk or Not a dunk; either answer removes it from this list.

Review Shot Types

Shots with no type yet, oldest first. Pick a player to start with, watch the clip, and pick the type. Skip hides one until you reload. The re-check views list tagged shots worth a second look.

Review Shots at the Same Moment

Two shots at the exact same video time are usually a miss and its putback. Watch the film and give the putback its real time. Same moment is right hides the pair until you reload.

Review Stopped-Early Games

Flag any game that was cut short, since a partial game throws off per-game panels like best games and power ranking comparisons. Season totals aren't affected.

Review Rebound Battles

Tag who was fighting for the rebound on each miss. One click tags one player; use Edit in the Shot Log for two. No contest means nobody was. Skip hides a row until you reload.

Data Management

Start New Season closes the current season: its games are kept but archived (see Include Past Seasons), and videos stored in this browser are cleared. Reset All Data deletes everything, roster included. Either one saves a backup file first.
